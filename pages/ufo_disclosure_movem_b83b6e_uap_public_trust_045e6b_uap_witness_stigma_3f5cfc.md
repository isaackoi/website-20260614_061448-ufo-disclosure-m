## Why witnesses hesitate to report

NASA’s study team did not argue that every unexplained sighting is extraordinary. Instead, it argued that scientific investigation depends on people feeling safe enough to report observations without fear of embarrassment, professional consequences, or public ridicule. The report specifically highlighted stigma as a barrier to gathering the information needed for serious analysis. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://science.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/uap-independent-study-team-final-report.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.nasa.gov">[NASA Science]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA ScienceIndependent Study Team ReportNASA&#x27;s very involvement in UAP will play a vital role in reducing stigma associated with UAP rep...</span></span></span>

For decades, UFO reports occupied a cultural space associated with conspiracy theories, hoaxes, and sensational [media]({{ 'media/' | relative_url }}) coverage. That history created incentives for silence. Commercial pilots, military personnel, air traffic professionals, and [researchers]({{ 'researchers/' | relative_url }}) often faced the possibility that reporting an unusual sighting could damage their credibility. During NASA's public discussions, participants noted that stigma made it difficult not only to obtain reports but also to recruit academic collaborators willing to work openly on the subject. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.nature.com/articles/s41599-024-03351-4" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nature.com">[Nature]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nature.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UAP. The issue is especially significant in aviation. Pilots are trained observers operating in safety-critical environments, yet several public discussions around UAP have highlighted concerns that reporting unusual encounters can be perceived as a threat to professional reputation. Former naval aviator Ryan Graves has publicly estimated that the overwhelming majority of pilot sightings go unreported, suggesting that investigators may be seeing only a small fraction of potentially relevant observations. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.flyingmag.com/unpacking-pentagon-latest-uap-release/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: flyingmag.com">[FLYING Magazine]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">flyingmag.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">unpacking pentagon latest uap release</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">FLYING MagazineUnpacking the Pentagon&#x27;s Latest UAP Release11 May 2026 — Graves during a 2023 Congressional hearing estimated that 95 perc...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 2026</span></span></span>

NASA therefore treated witness reluctance not as a cultural curiosity but as a practical obstacle to evidence collection. A report that is never filed cannot be compared with radar data, weather records, satellite imagery, or other observations. Once a witness decides not to speak, that information is effectively removed from the investigative process. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://science.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/uap-independent-study-team-final-report.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.nasa.gov">[NASA Science]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA ScienceIndependent Study Team ReportNASA&#x27;s very involvement in UAP will play a vital role in reducing stigma associated with UAP rep...</span></span></span>

## How stigma weakens data quality

The common assumption is that UAP investigations suffer from a lack of technology. NASA’s study suggested a different problem: many investigations begin with incomplete reporting.

The agency repeatedly stressed that current UAP research is limited by the scarcity of high-quality observations. Witness accounts exist, but they are often inconsistent, poorly documented, or detached from supporting measurements. Scientists cannot easily evaluate an event if they lack precise timing, location information, sensor metadata, environmental conditions, or corroborating observations. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.rev.com/transcripts/unidentified-anomalous-phenomena-independent-study-report-from-nasa-transcript" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: rev.com">[Rev]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">rev.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UAP Independent Study Report from NASAUAP, as Bill just eloquently said, are one of our planet&#x27;s greatest mysteries, and it&#x27;s really d...</span></span></span> <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://science.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/uap-independent-study-team-final-report.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.nasa.gov">[NASA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA ScienceIndependent Study Team ReportNASA&#x27;s very involvement in UAP will play a vital role in reducing stigma associated with UAP rep...</span></span></span>

Stigma worsens this problem in several ways:

* **Reports arrive late.** Witnesses may wait weeks, months, or years before speaking publicly, making verification difficult.
* **Details are lost.** People who fear ridicule may provide only partial accounts or avoid formal reporting channels.
* **Corroboration disappears.** Multiple witnesses may independently choose not to report the same event.
* **Scientists lose context.** Without structured reporting, investigators cannot match sightings against radar records, aircraft movements, weather data, or astronomical conditions. NASA Science <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://globalnews.ca/news/9737881/ufo-nasa-panel-meeting-data/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: globalnews.ca">[Global News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">globalnews.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ufo nasa panel meeting data</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO study is limited by data gaps, lingering stigma, NASA...31 May 2023 — “The current existing data and eyewitness reports alone are in...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 2023</span></span></span>

This creates a paradox. Public discussions often focus on the small number of unresolved cases, while investigators are frequently struggling with a much more basic problem: insufficient information. NASA’s study repeatedly returned to the conclusion that better evidence, not stronger speculation, is the missing ingredient. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.rev.com/transcripts/unidentified-anomalous-phenomena-independent-study-report-from-nasa-transcript" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: rev.com">[Rev]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">rev.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UAP Independent Study Report from NASAUAP, as Bill just eloquently said, are one of our planet&#x27;s greatest mysteries, and it&#x27;s really d...</span></span></span> <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-to-release-discuss-unidentified-anomalous-phenomena-report/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nasa.gov">[NASA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">to release discuss unidentified anomalous phenomena report</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA to Release, Discuss Unidentified Anomalous...12 Sept 2023 — NASA defines UAP as observations of events in the sky that cannot be id...</span></span></span>

In this sense, stigma functions as a data-quality issue. Even if every unexplained sighting eventually turns out to have a conventional explanation, suppressed reporting still prevents investigators from reaching that conclusion efficiently. Conversely, if genuinely unusual events exist, under-reporting makes them harder to identify. Either way, the evidence base becomes weaker. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://science.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/uap-independent-study-team-final-report.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.nasa.gov">[NASA Science]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA ScienceIndependent Study Team ReportNASA&#x27;s very involvement in UAP will play a vital role in reducing stigma associated with UAP rep...</span></span></span>

## The missing witness pipeline

NASA's warning implicitly identified a structural gap: there is no mature, trusted civilian pipeline for collecting, standardising, and analysing witness reports.

Many scientific fields have established reporting systems. Meteor observations, earthquake reports, disease surveillance, aviation safety incidents, and severe weather events all rely on channels that encourage routine reporting without social penalty. UAP reporting has historically lacked an equivalent framework. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://science.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/uap-independent-study-team-final-report.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.nasa.gov">[NASA Science]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA ScienceIndependent Study Team ReportNASA&#x27;s very involvement in UAP will play a vital role in reducing stigma associated with UAP rep...</span></span></span>

The consequence is a fragmented flow of information. Witnesses may choose among social media posts, journalists, private organisations, government portals, aviation channels, or no reporting at all. Data arrives in different formats, with different standards, and often without the metadata needed for scientific analysis. ## Why the stigma question matters for public trust

The witness pipeline sits at the intersection of evidence and institutional credibility. If reporting systems discourage participation, institutions receive poor-quality information and struggle to provide convincing answers. When those answers appear incomplete, public suspicion grows.

NASA’s study therefore linked stigma directly to the quality of future investigations. Its argument was not that witnesses should automatically be believed. Rather, it was that people should feel able to report observations without social or professional penalties so that claims can be examined properly.
