## Introduction

Within the [UFO disclosure movement]({{ 'ufo-disclosure-movement/' | relative_url }}), some of the most consequential claims are not about a particular sighting but about alleged hidden programmes: purported retrieval efforts, reverse-engineering projects, contractor activities, or restricted funding streams that witnesses believe were concealed from lawful oversight. Because such allegations may involve classified systems and special access programmes, Congress cannot simply rely on public testimony or [media]({{ 'media/' | relative_url }}) reports. A separate reporting route was created so that authorised witnesses can make protected disclosures through government channels, allowing lawmakers to be notified when allegations concern activities that may not have been properly reported to Congress. The key question is not whether any particular claim is true, but how such claims are supposed to travel from a protected witness to congressional oversight bodies. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&amp;num=0&amp;req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title50-section3373b" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uscode.house.gov">[U.S. Code]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uscode.house.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Code50 USC 3373b: Unidentified anomalous phenomena...(B) Congressional notification. ## Why alleged hidden programmes need a separate route

Ordinary UAP incident reporting and allegations about secret programmes raise different oversight problems. A pilot reporting an unusual object is primarily providing observational information. By contrast, a witness claiming knowledge of a restricted programme may be describing classified budgets, compartmented activities, contractor work, or special access arrangements.

Congress has long accepted that some national-security activities require [secrecy]({{ 'secrecy/' | relative_url }}). However, it also expects that appropriately authorised committees are informed about programmes falling within their jurisdiction. The concern behind the UAP whistleblower framework is that a witness could allege the existence of a restricted activity that was never explicitly disclosed to the congressional committees responsible for oversight. In that situation, the issue becomes one of governance and accountability rather than merely anomaly investigation. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=%28title%3A50+section%3A3373" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uscode.house.gov">[U.S. Code]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uscode.house.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">house.gov50 USC 3373: Establishment of office, organizational...Establishment of office, organizational structure, and authorities to ad...</span></span></span>

This distinction explains why the reporting statute covers not only UAP sightings but also information concerning UAP-related research, development, testing, retrieval, analysis, security activities, and contractor involvement. The law was designed to capture programme-level allegations that might otherwise remain inside classified compartments. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&amp;num=0&amp;req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title50-section3373b" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uscode.house.gov">[U.S. Code]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uscode.house.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Code50 USC 3373b: Unidentified anomalous phenomena...(B) Congressional notification. His public claims drew attention because they centred on alleged programmes and withheld information rather than on a personal observation of an unexplained object. ## How the 72-hour reporting trigger is supposed to work

The most important mechanism is contained in 50 U.S.C. § 3373b. Under that framework, an authorised disclosure is first received through the designated reporting process rather than being immediately transmitted to Congress. Officials then assess the disclosure and determine whether it relates to a restricted access activity, special access programme, or compartmented access programme. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=%28title%3A50+section%3A3373" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uscode.house.gov">[U.S. Code]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uscode.house.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">house.gov50 USC 3373: Establishment of office, organizational...Establishment of office, organizational structure, and authorities to ad...</span></span></span>

The statute creates a specific escalation requirement. If officials determine that the authorised disclosure concerns a restricted or compartmented activity that has **not been explicitly and clearly reported** to the congressional defence committees or congressional intelligence committees, the Secretary of Defense must report the disclosure to those committees and congressional leadership within 72 hours. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=%28title%3A50+section%3A3373" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uscode.house.gov">[U.S. Code]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uscode.house.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">house.gov50 USC 3373: Establishment of office, organizational...Establishment of office, organizational structure, and authorities to ad...</span></span></span>

This provision is significant because it creates a formal bridge between protected witness testimony and congressional oversight. The trigger is not proof that a hidden programme exists. Rather, the trigger is the determination that the disclosure concerns an allegedly unreported restricted activity. The law therefore attempts to prevent programme allegations from becoming trapped inside the same compartment that the witness is describing. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=%28title%3A50+section%3A3373" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uscode.house.gov">[U.S. Code]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uscode.house.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">house.gov50 USC 3373: Establishment of office, organizational...Establishment of office, organizational structure, and authorities to ad...</span></span></span>

## Which committees are positioned to receive protected claims

The congressional destination for these notifications is shaped by committee jurisdiction. The law specifically identifies the congressional defence committees and congressional intelligence committees as primary recipients when an allegedly unreported restricted activity is involved. Congressional leadership is also included in the notification requirement. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=%28title%3A50+section%3A3373" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uscode.house.gov">[U.S. Code]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uscode.house.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">house.gov50 USC 3373: Establishment of office, organizational...Establishment of office, organizational structure, and authorities to ad...</span></span></span>

This structure reflects the nature of the allegations. Claims involving special access programmes, intelligence compartments, classified technology, or contractor activity typically fall within the oversight responsibilities of defence and intelligence committees rather than committees focused on science or public records. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=%28title%3A50+section%3A3373" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uscode.house.gov">[U.S. Code]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uscode.house.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">house.gov50 USC 3373: Establishment of office, organizational...Establishment of office, organizational structure, and authorities to ad...</span></span></span>

The House Office of the Whistleblower Ombuds also identifies committees such as Intelligence, Armed Services, and Oversight as relevant to UAP whistleblowing issues. ## What this route can and cannot accomplish

The congressional-notification pathway is often misunderstood. It does not automatically validate a whistleblower's allegations. Congress may receive a protected disclosure and ultimately conclude that the underlying claims are unsupported, mistaken, or already known through other channels.

What the route does accomplish is procedural. It increases the likelihood that serious programme allegations are reviewed by officials with oversight authority rather than remaining confined within the organisation being accused of withholding information. In governance terms, the mechanism is designed to address a reporting failure, not to predetermine the outcome of an investigation. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=%28title%3A50+section%3A3373" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uscode.house.gov">[U.S. Code]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uscode.house.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">house.gov50 USC 3373: Establishment of office, organizational...Establishment of office, organizational structure, and authorities to ad...</span></span></span>
