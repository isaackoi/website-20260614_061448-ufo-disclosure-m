## What problem the proposal tries to solve

Graves has repeatedly argued that aviation suffers from a reporting gap. According to this view, pilots encounter objects or events they cannot readily identify, yet many incidents never enter a formal database because witnesses are unsure where to report them or worry about how colleagues, employers, regulators, or medical evaluators might react. The issue is presented as a safety problem rather than evidence for any particular explanation. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://aviationweek.com/podcasts/check-6/podcast-understanding-uap-aerospace-safety-concern" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aviationweek.com">[Aviation Week]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aviationweek.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">podcast understanding uap aerospace safety concern</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Aviation WeekUnderstanding UAP As An Aerospace Safety Concern25 Oct 2024 — Former F/A-18 pilot Ryan Graves who leads Americans for Safe A...</span></span></span>

The Safe Airspace for Americans Act was introduced to create a standard federal process for civilian aviation reporting. Early versions of the proposal would allow civilian pilots and other aviation personnel to submit UAP reports through established aviation-safety mechanisms, including NASA's Aviation Safety Reporting System (ASRS), while also providing protections against retaliation and encouraging systematic collection of data. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.safeaerospace.org/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: safeaerospace.org">[Safe Aerospace]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">safeaerospace.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Safe AerospaceAmericans for Safe AerospaceAmericans for Safe Aerospace - A military pilot-led 501(c)(3) nonprofit and the world&#x27;s largest...</span></span></span> <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://thedebrief.org/new-bipartisan-bill-aims-to-strengthen-uap-reporting-from-civilian-pilots-and-aviation-personnel/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thedebrief.org">[The Debrief]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thedebrief.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">New Bipartisan Bill Aims to Strengthen UAP Reporting from...11 Jan 2024 — A new bill, the &#x27;Safe Airspace for Americans Act&#x27; aims to enco...</span></span></span>

Supporters argue that this approach mirrors established safety culture in aviation. Modern accident prevention relies on voluntary reporting systems that encourage disclosure of hazards, mistakes, and near misses. The theory behind the bill is that unexplained aerial encounters should be treated similarly: witnesses should be encouraged to report first, while investigators determine later whether the event involved a drone, balloon, sensor anomaly, atmospheric phenomenon, misidentification, or something genuinely unresolved. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://aiaa.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/AIAA-UAPIOC-Opinion-Paper-UAP-Occupational-Safety-Reporting_ForPublication_kb.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aiaa.org">[AIAA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aiaa.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Shaping the future of aerospace ADDRESSING THE UNKNOWN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">2. ## Who would be protected by the reporting pathway

Although public discussion often centres on pilots, the legislation has been framed more broadly. Legislative summaries and supporting advocacy materials describe reporting pathways for civilian aviation personnel, a category extending beyond cockpit crews. Depending on the version discussed, reporting could involve pilots, air traffic controllers, flight attendants, dispatchers, maintenance personnel, airport staff, and other aviation workers who observe unusual aerial events during their professional duties. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.safeaerospace.org/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: safeaerospace.org">[Safe Aerospace]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">safeaerospace.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Safe AerospaceAmericans for Safe AerospaceAmericans for Safe Aerospace - A military pilot-led 501(c)(3) nonprofit and the world&#x27;s largest...</span></span></span>

The protection element is central. Americans for Safe Aerospace, the organisation led by Graves, has argued that witnesses need assurance that filing a report will not expose them to retaliation, reputational damage, licensing consequences, or threats to their employment. The organisation explicitly endorses the Safe Airspace for Americans Act because it links reporting to anti-retaliation protections and seeks to normalise professional disclosure of UAP encounters. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.safeaerospace.org/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: safeaerospace.org">[Safe Aerospace]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">safeaerospace.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Safe AerospaceAmericans for Safe AerospaceAmericans for Safe Aerospace - A military pilot-led 501(c)(3) nonprofit and the world&#x27;s largest...</span></span></span>

The proposed protections are important because aviation careers are highly regulated. Pilots depend on medical certification, recurrent training, employer evaluations, and regulatory compliance. Even if formal punishment is unlikely, witnesses may fear being viewed as unreliable or unprofessional. Supporters of the bill contend that a protected reporting framework reduces those concerns and increases the likelihood that useful safety information reaches investigators. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.safeaerospace.org/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: safeaerospace.org">[Safe Aerospace]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">safeaerospace.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Safe AerospaceAmericans for Safe AerospaceAmericans for Safe Aerospace - A military pilot-led 501(c)(3) nonprofit and the world&#x27;s largest...</span></span></span>

Americans for Safe Aerospace has attempted to demonstrate this need through its own confidential reporting network, which accepts reports from pilots, veterans, and civilians and publishes de-identified accounts after review. The organisation presents this as evidence that witnesses are more willing to come forward when confidentiality and respectful handling are available. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.safeaerospace.org/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: safeaerospace.org">[Safe Aerospace]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">safeaerospace.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Safe AerospaceAmericans for Safe AerospaceAmericans for Safe Aerospace - A military pilot-led 501(c)(3) nonprofit and the world&#x27;s largest...</span></span></span>

## Why civilian reporting matters to the safety argument

The policy's importance lies in its attempt to expand reporting beyond military channels. Much public attention in the UAP debate has focused on military encounters, but civilian aviation covers a far larger operational environment. Commercial airliners, business aircraft, air ambulances, flight schools, agricultural operators, and private pilots collectively generate enormous amounts of observational data.

Advocates argue that a standardised reporting framework would make it easier to identify patterns. A single report may be ambiguous. Hundreds of reports collected through consistent procedures can potentially reveal recurring locations, common misidentifications, drone incursions, equipment issues, or other hazards requiring attention. That logic closely follows conventional aviation-safety practice, where trend analysis often matters more than individual anecdotes. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://aiaa.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/AIAA-UAPIOC-Opinion-Paper-UAP-Occupational-Safety-Reporting_ForPublication_kb.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aiaa.org">[AIAA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aiaa.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Shaping the future of aerospace ADDRESSING THE UNKNOWN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">2. ## Where legislation cannot replace investigation

The Safe Airspace for Americans Act addresses reporting, not explanation. Even if enacted exactly as supporters envision, it would not determine what any reported object actually was.

A protected reporting system can increase the number and quality of reports, but it cannot by itself verify observations, resolve contradictory accounts, or distinguish between unusual technology, ordinary objects, sensor artefacts, and genuinely unexplained cases. Those tasks still require technical investigation, access to radar and [sensor data]({{ 'sensor-data/' | relative_url }}), interagency cooperation, and analytical review. Agencies such as the U.S. government's All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO) were created for that investigative role. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Home The official website for the All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AARO HomeThe official website for the All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO)...</span></span></span>
