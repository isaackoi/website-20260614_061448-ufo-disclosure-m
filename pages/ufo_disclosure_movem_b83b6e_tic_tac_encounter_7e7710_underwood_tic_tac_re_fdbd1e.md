## The Pilot Who Named the Tic Tac

The label “Tic Tac” is now inseparable from the Nimitz case, yet it did not originate in government reports or [media]({{ 'media/' | relative_url }}) coverage. Underwood has stated that he was the person who began using the nickname because the object's appearance reminded him of the white mint confection. ## How Underwood Recorded the Later Video

One of the most commonly misunderstood aspects of the Nimitz incident is that the famous FLIR1 infrared video was not recorded during Fravor's reported close visual encounter. After Fravor's flight returned to the carrier and discussed the unusual event, another aircraft launched with more advanced sensor equipment. Underwood was aboard that later mission and used the aircraft's Forward Looking Infrared (FLIR) targeting system to track the object. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pentagon_UFO_videos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Pentagon UFO videos</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Pentagon UFO videos</span></span></span>

Underwood has explained that his priority was not visual observation but sensor collection. Rather than concentrating on looking out of the cockpit, he focused on operating and maintaining the lock of the targeting system. He later said that he wanted to bring back as much recorded information as possible so intelligence personnel could analyse it afterwards. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UFO_photographs" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFO photographs</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO photographs</span></span></span> <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pentagon_UFO_videos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Pentagon UFO videos</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Pentagon UFO videos</span></span></span>

This distinction is important because it clarifies what the video actually represents. The footage is evidence that a military sensor tracked something that crews could not immediately identify. It is not a complete recording of every manoeuvre described by earlier witnesses, nor does it independently verify every claim later associated with the broader Nimitz story. Underwood's role therefore links the case's eyewitness component to its sensor component without collapsing the two into a single piece of evidence. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UFO_photographs" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFO photographs</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO photographs</span></span></span>

## Why the Name Stuck in Public Memory

Many UFO incidents fade because they lack a memorable narrative. The Nimitz case developed one. A strange white object, experienced military personnel, an infrared recording and the simple phrase “Tic Tac” combined into a story that could easily be retold.

Underwood's contribution was central to that process. His recording became the visual artefact attached to the event, while the nickname supplied a shared vocabulary. Long before governments began using the broader term “UAP” (Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena), the public could instantly understand what witnesses meant when they referred to the “Tic Tac”. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://gvwire.com/2019/12/20/navy-pilot-who-filmed-the-tic-tac-ufo-speaks-it-wasnt-behaving-by-the-normal-laws-of-physics/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gvwire.com">[GV Wire]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gvwire.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GV WireNavy Pilot Who Filmed the &#x27;Tic Tac&#x27; UFO Speaks: &#x27;It Wasn&#x27;t...20 Dec 2019 — Chad Underwood recorded a bizarre and erratic UFO — no...</span></span></span>

The result was unusual. Many disclosure-era discussions revolve around classified documents, radar data or technical reports. The Tic Tac case instead acquired a human-scale identity tied to one memorable image and one memorable name. Underwood helped create both.

## What His Caution Says About Responsible Interpretation

Perhaps the most revealing aspect of Underwood's public comments is what he has not claimed.

Although he has described the tracked object as behaving in ways that appeared extraordinary and inconsistent with ordinary aircraft performance, he has generally avoided declaring that it was an alien vehicle. In interviews, he has emphasised that the object was unidentified rather than definitively identified as something extraterrestrial. He has also expressed discomfort with being automatically associated with claims about aliens simply because he recorded the video. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://news.sky.com/story/it-was-behaving-erratically-us-navy-pilot-speaks-out-about-ufo-sighting-15-years-on-11891191" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: news.sky.com">[Sky News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">news.sky.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">It was going from like 50,000ft to, you know, 100ft in like seconds, which is not possible.&quot; Mr Underwood said...Read more...</span></span></span> <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://nymag.com/intelligencer/2019/12/tic-tac-ufo-video-q-and-a-with-navy-pilot-chad-underwood.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nymag.com">[New York Magazine]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nymag.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">tic tac ufo video q and a with navy pilot chad underwood</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">New York Magazine&#x27;Tic Tac&#x27; UFO Video: Q&amp;A With Navy Pilot Chad Underwood19 Dec 2019 — Navy pilot who filmed the &#x27;Tic Tac&#x27; UFO speaks: &#x27;It...</span></span></span>

That position creates an important contrast within the UFO debate. Some advocates view the Nimitz encounter as evidence of advanced non-human technology.
