## Introduction

However, the report did not end the dispute. For supporters of Grusch, the key question is not simply what AARO concluded, but whether AARO had access to every compartmented programme, witness, and classified record needed to test the allegations. For sceptics, the report represents the strongest public institutional rebuttal yet produced. ## What AARO said it found

AARO’s March 2024 historical review was designed as a government-wide examination of claims about unidentified anomalous phenomena (UAPs), including the specific narrative that recovered extraterrestrial technology had been secretly studied for decades. After reviewing historical records, interviewing witnesses, and examining both classified and unclassified material, the office stated that it found no empirical evidence that the US government or private [contractors]({{ 'contractors/' | relative_url }}) had been reverse-engineering extraterrestrial technology. U.S. Department of War <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://rollcall.com/2024/03/08/no-evidence-of-extraterrestrial-technology-report-finds/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: rollcall.com">[Roll Call]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">rollcall.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Roll CallNo evidence of extraterrestrial technology, report findsAccording to AARO&#x27;s assessment, most UAP sightings are ordinary objects...</span></span></span>

The report addressed the same broad narrative that underpins Grusch’s public allegations. AARO concluded that named programmes described by interviewees either did not exist, were misidentified conventional classified programmes, or traced back to misunderstood historical activities rather than hidden non-human technology projects. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page%3AAARO_Historical_Record_Report_Volume_1_2024.pdf/9" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.wikisource.org">[Wikisource]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.wikisource.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Page:AARO Historical Record Report Volume 1 2024</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Page:AARO Historical Record Report Volume 1 2024.pdf/94 May 2024 — AARO assesses that all of the named and described alleged hi...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 2024</span></span></span>

One of the report’s strongest passages directly challenged the crash-retrieval story. AARO wrote that it had no evidence supporting the reverse-engineering narrative presented by interviewees and that it had disproved many of the specific claims it examined. It also reported that alleged pieces of “off-world” material submitted for review were found to be ordinary terrestrial substances without extraordinary properties. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page%3AAARO_Historical_Record_Report_Volume_1_2024.pdf/10" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.wikisource.org">[Wikisource]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.wikisource.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Page:AARO Historical Record Report Volume 1 2024.pdf/104 May 2024 — AARO has no evidence for the USG reverse-engineering narrative...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 2024</span></span></span>

The report additionally argued that some UFO legends developed through a mixture of misunderstanding, compartmented defence activities, folklore, and repeated retelling. It described a process of “circular reporting” in which claims were repeated among believers and later cited as independent confirmation despite tracing back to the same underlying stories. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://phys.org/news/2024-03-pentagon-evidence-secret-alien-tech.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: phys.org">[Phys.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">phys.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">2024 03 pentagon evidence secret alien tech</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Pentagon says no evidence of secret US work on alien techMar 8, 2024 — AARO said the inaccurate reverse-engineering claims are &quot;in large...</span></span></span>

## Why access to compartments matters

The most important limitation identified by critics is not a factual error inside the report but a question about investigative reach.

Grusch’s allegations have always centred on highly restricted Special Access Programmes and other compartmented activities. His argument is not that ordinary defence archives contain proof of recovered non-human technology, but that information is hidden inside unusually protected structures. Because of that, supporters argue that a negative finding only carries full weight if investigators had unrestricted access to every relevant compartment and every potentially knowledgeable witness. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.pbs.org/newshour/politics/watch-live-house-oversight-committee-probes-ufos-and-wider-implications" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pbs.org">[PBS]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pbs.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WATCH: Whistleblower tells Congress the U.S. is concealing...Retired Maj. David Grusch&#x27;s highly anticipated testimony before a House...</span></span></span>

This creates a verification problem. If AARO was granted complete access, then its findings become powerful evidence against the crash-retrieval narrative. If important compartments remained inaccessible, then believers can argue that the investigation was unable to test the central allegation. The public generally lacks the information needed to independently determine which interpretation is correct. Much of the dispute therefore shifts from the UFO claim itself to questions of bureaucratic access, oversight authority, and institutional [trust]({{ 'trust/' | relative_url }}). <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://media.defense.gov/2024/Mar/08/2003409233/-1/-1/0/DOPSR-2024-0263-AARO-HISTORICAL-RECORD-REPORT-VOLUME-1-2024.PDF" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: media.defense.gov">[U.S. Department of War]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">media.defense.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">DOPSR 2024 0263 AARO HISTORICAL RECORD REPORT VOLUME 1 2024</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Department of WarAARO Historical Record Report Volume 1Mar 8, 2024 — AARO assesses that the inaccurate claim that the USG is reverse-engi...</span></span></span>

AARO’s own position was that it conducted a broad review across government records and interviews and found no evidence supporting the alleged programmes. The office presented its conclusions as the product of extensive access rather than a limited review. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://media.defense.gov/2024/Mar/08/2003409233/-1/-1/0/DOPSR-2024-0263-AARO-HISTORICAL-RECORD-REPORT-VOLUME-1-2024.PDF" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: media.defense.gov">[U.S. Department of War]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">media.defense.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">DOPSR 2024 0263 AARO HISTORICAL RECORD REPORT VOLUME 1 2024</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Department of WarAARO Historical Record Report Volume 1Mar 8, 2024 — AARO assesses that the inaccurate claim that the USG is reverse-engi...</span></span></span>

Yet critics of the report argued that proving a secret programme does not exist is inherently more difficult than disproving a specific document or witness account. In practical terms, the argument became less about UFOs and more about the limits of internal government auditing when the subject is alleged to be hidden inside classified structures.

## How supporters and sceptics read the same report

The report is notable because opposing sides often cite it while drawing opposite conclusions.

### The sceptical reading

For sceptics, AARO's report is the strongest official rebuttal yet produced. It was commissioned by Congress, drew upon government records, addressed the reverse-engineering narrative directly, and concluded that no evidence supports claims of recovered alien craft or secret exploitation programmes. From this perspective, Grusch’s allegations remain unverified because no physical evidence, documents, or publicly testable materials have emerged despite extensive investigation. ### The disclosure-supporter reading

Supporters of Grusch often interpret the report differently. They argue that AARO was effectively reviewing allegations against the same national security system that allegedly generated them. From that perspective, a negative conclusion is not surprising and does not automatically invalidate whistleblower testimony. Some critics also contend that AARO relied too heavily on official records and insufficiently on witnesses who claim direct involvement in alleged programmes. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.theguardian.com/world/2024/mar/08/pentagon-ufo-report-hiding-aliens" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theguardian.com">[The Guardian]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theguardian.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Conducted by the All-Domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO), the investigation reviewed historical data and conducted interviews with of...</span></span></span>

## What the report changed in the Grusch debate

Before AARO’s historical review, the public discussion was dominated by allegations. After the report, there was finally a detailed official document aimed specifically at the crash-retrieval and reverse-engineering narrative.

That shifted the burden of argument. The central question became less “Has the government responded?” and more “Is the government’s response trustworthy and complete?” AARO’s report provided the disclosure movement with its clearest official counterargument, but it did not eliminate the underlying disagreement about [secrecy]({{ 'secrecy/' | relative_url }}), access, and oversight. [U.S. Department of War+2The Guardian]

As a result, the report occupies a unique place in the [UFO disclosure movement]({{ 'ufo-disclosure-movement/' | relative_url }}).
