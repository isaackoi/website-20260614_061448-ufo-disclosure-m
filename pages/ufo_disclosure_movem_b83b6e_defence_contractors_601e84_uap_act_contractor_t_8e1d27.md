## Introduction

The most unusual feature of the proposed Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena (UAP) [Disclosure Act]({{ 'disclosure-act/' | relative_url }}) was not that it discussed UFOs. It was that it treated the possibility of privately held UAP-related material as a governance problem requiring legal mechanisms, even without proving that such material actually exists. The bill's architects did not write legislation declaring that non-human technology had been recovered. Instead, they wrote language aimed at a scenario in which relevant material, records, or technologies might be held by private entities beyond normal government custody and oversight. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.democrats.senate.gov/newsroom/press-releases/schumer-rounds-introduce-new-legislation-to-declassify-government-records-related-to-unidentified-anomalous-phenomena-and-ufos_modeled-after-jfk-assassination-records-collection-act--as-an-amendment-to-ndaa" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: democrats.senate.gov">[Senate Democratic Leadership]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">democrats.senate.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Senate Democratic LeadershipSchumer, Rounds Introduce New Legislation To Declassify...Jul 14, 2023 — The legislation introduced as an am...</span></span></span>

## How the Bill Defined Private Controlling Authorities

A key feature of the Senate proposal was its attempt to look beyond traditional government archives. The legislation repeatedly referred to records, technologies, and evidence that could exist outside ordinary agency custody. Rather than assuming that all relevant material would be sitting in a federal warehouse or military facility, the bill contemplated situations in which information or physical objects might be controlled by private persons, corporations, or other entities. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.democrats.senate.gov/newsroom/press-releases/schumer-rounds-introduce-new-legislation-to-declassify-government-records-related-to-unidentified-anomalous-phenomena-and-ufos_modeled-after-jfk-assassination-records-collection-act--as-an-amendment-to-ndaa" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: democrats.senate.gov">[Senate Democratic Leadership]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">democrats.senate.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Senate Democratic LeadershipSchumer, Rounds Introduce New Legislation To Declassify...Jul 14, 2023 — The legislation introduced as an am...</span></span></span>

This mattered because many of the most prominent allegations in modern UAP debates involve contractors rather than government departments. Former intelligence official David Grusch and other disclosure advocates argued that any legacy retrieval or reverse-engineering efforts would likely have been placed inside highly compartmentalised contractor environments. Whether those claims are true remains disputed, but the legislative language shows that senators considered the possibility important enough to address procedurally. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.democrats.senate.gov/newsroom/press-releases/schumer-rounds-introduce-new-legislation-to-declassify-government-records-related-to-unidentified-anomalous-phenomena-and-ufos_modeled-after-jfk-assassination-records-collection-act--as-an-amendment-to-ndaa" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: democrats.senate.gov">[Senate Democratic Leadership]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">democrats.senate.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Senate Democratic LeadershipSchumer, Rounds Introduce New Legislation To Declassify...Jul 14, 2023 — The legislation introduced as an am...</span></span></span>

The bill therefore treated private control as a potential obstacle to transparency. Its structure implied a concern that records and materials funded, generated, or acquired through government activity could become effectively insulated from public scrutiny if held under contractor ownership, proprietary restrictions, or special-access arrangements. Importantly, the legislation did not state that such hidden material existed; it established mechanisms that would apply if it did. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.democrats.senate.gov/newsroom/press-releases/schumer-rounds-introduce-new-legislation-to-declassify-government-records-related-to-unidentified-anomalous-phenomena-and-ufos_modeled-after-jfk-assassination-records-collection-act--as-an-amendment-to-ndaa" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: democrats.senate.gov">[Senate Democratic Leadership]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">democrats.senate.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Senate Democratic LeadershipSchumer, Rounds Introduce New Legislation To Declassify...Jul 14, 2023 — The legislation introduced as an am...</span></span></span>

## Why Eminent Domain Appeared in the Proposal

The most discussed provision was the proposal that the federal government exercise eminent domain over recovered technologies of unknown origin and biological evidence of non-human intelligence controlled by private persons or entities. In ordinary public discussion, this clause was often portrayed as evidence that Congress believed contractors possessed alien technology. The legislative logic was more cautious. The clause functioned as a contingency mechanism. If such materials existed and were privately held, the government would have a clear legal pathway to obtain them for review and disclosure decisions rather than relying on voluntary cooperation. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://amendments-rules.house.gov/amendments/GARCRO_115_xml240529153551283.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: amendments-rules.house.gov">[House Rules Committee]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">amendments-rules.house.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Rules Committee Subtitle D—Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena Disclosure</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">14. From an implementation perspective, the provision solved a practical problem. A disclosure regime modelled on the JFK Records Act could compel government agencies to produce records, but it would have much less leverage over privately controlled material.
